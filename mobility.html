Hips & Hip Flexors
12 minutes · 4 exercises · cycling's #1 problem area
Low Lunge Hip Flexor Stretch
Knee on a folded blanket, gentle forward shift — stop before any discomfort
3 min
60 sec each side
Kneel on a folded blanket or yoga mat for cushioning. Put your right foot forward, left knee on the floor. Sit upright — don't lean forward. Gently shift your hips forward just a little, until you feel a mild pull at the front of the left hip. Even a tiny shift counts. Hold there, breathing slowly. No need to go deep — a gentle sensation is plenty. Switch sides. If kneeling is uncomfortable, use a chair: stand near the chair, step one foot back, and lightly bend the front knee instead.

Pigeon Pose
Use a chair or lie on your back — no floor flexibility needed
4 min
90 sec each side
Beginner version (chair): Sit in a firm chair. Cross your right ankle over your left knee, flex your right foot gently. Sit tall and feel a mild stretch in the right outer hip — that's enough. Floor version: Lie on your back. Cross your right ankle over your left knee. Keep both feet flexed. Either stay here, or if comfortable, lift your left leg toward your chest. Never force this one. If you feel knee pain rather than hip stretch, uncross and try the chair version. Hold whichever version 60–90 seconds each side.

90/90 Hip Stretch
Sit on the floor with support behind you if needed
3 min
90 sec each side
Sit on the floor. Bend both knees to 90 degrees — one in front, one to the side. If sitting upright feels hard, sit on a folded blanket or firm cushion to raise your hips slightly — this makes it much easier. Just sit tall here; you don't need to fold forward at all to get the benefit. Simply being in this position stretches the outer hip. If the position feels impossible, stay with the chair pigeon version above instead — it trains the same muscles.

Standing Figure-4 Stretch
Stand near a wall — no squat required
2 min
60 sec each side
Stand next to a wall and place one hand on it. Cross your right ankle over your left knee and flex your right foot. You don't need to squat at all — just standing with the ankle crossed gives a stretch. If you want a bit more, barely bend the standing knee. The wall is there to hold your full weight if needed. Alternatively, do this sitting in a chair (same position as the chair pigeon pose above) and lean forward from the hips slightly.

Lower Back
10 minutes · 4 exercises · decompress the spine
Cat-Cow
On hands and knees — use a folded blanket under knees
2 min
10 slow cycles
Fold a blanket under your knees for cushioning. Start on hands and knees. Make the movements very small to start — just a slight dip and slight rounding. You don't need a big arch or a big curve. Inhale and let your belly drop a tiny bit (cow). Exhale and gently round your back a tiny bit (cat). As it loosens up over the weeks, the range will naturally grow. Do 10 slow cycles, breathing with each movement. Chair version: Sit upright in a chair, hands on knees. Inhale and arch your back slightly; exhale and round it. Same benefit, no floor needed.

Supine Knee-to-Chest
Lie on your back — this one is easy for any flexibility level
2 min
45 sec each side
Lie on your back — this is one of the most accessible stretches in the routine. Bend both knees and slowly pull one knee toward your chest. Hold your thigh rather than your shin if reaching the shin is hard — it works just as well. Pull only as far as feels comfortable. Rock gently side to side. Don't worry about straightening the other leg — keep both knees bent if that's easier on your back. Switch sides after 45 seconds. Finish by hugging both knees in together and rocking slowly left and right.

Supine Spinal Twist
Lie back, let one knee fall sideways — use a pillow if needed
4 min
90 sec each side
Lie on your back. Bend your right knee and let it fall slowly to the left side. Your knee does NOT need to reach the floor — place a pillow or folded blanket under it wherever it lands. Turn your head gently to the right. Arms relaxed. Just breathe. Even if your knee is floating 12 inches off the floor, the lower back is still rotating and releasing. Over weeks it will sink lower on its own. Hold 60–90 seconds. Come back to centre slowly, then repeat on the other side.

Child's Pose with Lateral Reach
Kneel on a blanket — hips don't need to reach heels
2 min
30 sec × 3 positions
Kneel on a folded blanket. You don't need to sit all the way back to your heels — go as far back as is comfortable and stop there. Walk your hands forward along the floor until you feel a gentle stretch in your lower back. If your forehead doesn't reach the floor, rest it on your stacked fists or a pillow. Hold centre 30 seconds. Then slowly walk both hands to the right — feel the stretch along the left side of your back. Hold 30 seconds. Return to centre, then walk to the left. No need to go far — even a small sideways reach creates the stretch.

Shoulders & Neck
8 minutes · 4 exercises · undo the handlebar hunch
Neck Half-Circles
Seated, tiny slow movements — less is more
2 min
20 sec per position
Sit in a chair. Make these movements very small and slow — the neck doesn't need a big range. Gently tilt your right ear toward your right shoulder — just a few inches is fine. Hold 20 seconds, breathing out. Slowly bring your chin toward your chest. Hold 20 seconds. Tilt your left ear to your left shoulder. Hold 20 seconds. Come back to centre. Never roll the head backwards — only forward and side to side. If you feel any dizziness or sharp pain, stop and just do gentle side tilts only. Do 3 slow arcs total.

Doorway Chest Opener
Stand in any doorway — lean in gently, no force needed
2 min
45 sec × 2
Stand in a doorway with your forearms resting on the frame, elbows at shoulder height. Don't lean far — just shift your weight forward slightly until you feel a mild pull across the front of your chest. Even standing upright with your arms on the frame gives a stretch. Hold 30–45 seconds, breathing slowly. Step back and repeat once more. No doorway? Stand tall, clasp your hands behind your lower back, and gently squeeze your shoulder blades together. That's the same stretch, no doorway needed.

Foam Roller Thoracic Extension
Sit in front of roller, lean back over it — let gravity do the work
2 min
30 sec per spot
Place your foam roller on the floor horizontally. Sit on the floor in front of it, knees bent. Gently lean back so the roller sits across your upper-mid back — roughly where your shoulder blades are, not your lower back. Support your head with your hands clasped behind your neck. Let gravity pull your upper back over the roller. Start with zero effort — just let it rest there and breathe. After 20–30 seconds, shuffle slightly up or down to hit a different spot. You can roll very slowly back and forth. This directly reverses the rounded-forward position from the handlebars and is more effective than thread the needle for cyclists.

Shoulder Rolls & Cross-Body Reach
Standing or seated — gentle rolls and easy arm pull
2 min
30 sec each side
Sit or stand — either works. Do 10 slow backward shoulder rolls. They don't need to be big — even small circles loosen things up. Then bring your right arm across your chest at shoulder height. Use your left hand to very gently guide it a little closer to your chest. Don't pull hard — just light pressure. You should feel a mild stretch in the back of the right shoulder. Keep the right shoulder down, not shrugged up. Hold 20–30 seconds. Switch sides. Finish with 5 more gentle backward rolls.

Full Body Finish
5 minutes · close the session
Savasana — Calves on Chair
Lie on your back, calves resting on a couch or chair seat
5 min
Passive hold
Pull a chair or use your couch. Lie on your back on the floor and rest your calves up on the seat — knees bent at roughly 90 degrees, thighs vertical. This is entirely passive — no effort required. Arms relaxed by your sides, palms up. Close your eyes and breathe slowly. Elevating the legs drains blood pooling from cycling, calms your nervous system, and gently decompresses the lower back. Far easier to get into than legs-up-the-wall and just as effective for recovery. Stay here the full 5 minutes — this is where your body processes the whole session.
